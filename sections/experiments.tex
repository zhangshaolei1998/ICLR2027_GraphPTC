\section{Experiments}

\subsection{Benchmarks and Setup}
\label{sec:experiments-setup}

We evaluate on seven tool-use benchmark settings that span retrieval, question answering, application control, and workflow automation.
We choose these benchmarks because they stress the cross-block dependency that GraphPTC targets along complementary axes, they range from short to long horizons, and each carries an executable or fixed-grader evaluation that scores an outcome against a reference.
\textbf{BrowseComp-Plus}~\citep{wei2025browsecomp} measures evidence-seeking browsing over a fixed corpus and reports answer accuracy.
\textbf{AppWorld}~\citep{trivedi2024appworld} is an executable world of everyday apps, and we use its normal and challenge splits scored by Task Goal Completion (TGC) and Scenario Goal Completion (SGC).
\textbf{Agent-Diff} measures stateful multi-step tasks with an assertion-based pass rate.
\textbf{FanOutQA}~\citep{zhu2024fanoutqa} measures multi-hop, multi-document question answering with a judge accuracy.
\textbf{FRAMES}~\citep{krishna2024frames} measures fact retrieval and reasoning with a judge accuracy.
\textbf{AutomationBench} measures business workflow automation with a task completion rate.
The two AppWorld splits give seven evaluation settings over six benchmarks, and with four backbones this forms $28$ model-setting pairs.

We instantiate three methods under one shared protocol.
\emph{Direct Tool Calling} uses native per-call function calling, \emph{PTC} uses programmatic tool calling with a persistent runtime and no graph, and \emph{GraphPTC} adds the dynamic execution graph of Section~\ref{sec:method}.
All three share the same tools, prompts, and budgets on each benchmark, and the feedback budget of GraphPTC is held fixed across all benchmarks and backbones, so a gain reflects the graph and not per-setting tuning.
The four backbones, GPT-5.6-Sol, Claude-Opus-5, DeepSeek-V4-Pro, and Kimi-K3, cover closed and open, dense and mixture-of-experts models.

\subsection{Main Results}

\textbf{Capability on Interactive Application Control.}\quad
We evaluate \method on the AppWorld normal and challenge splits to assess its \emph{long-horizon application control}, where a task drives many app calls whose write effects must persist and compose into a final goal.
As shown in Table~\ref{tab:main}, \method improves over both Direct Tool Calling and PTC on both splits for every backbone, and its margin over PTC grows from the normal split to the challenge split.
We attribute this pattern to modeling state as typed nodes, so a later block links to an earlier write instead of re-deriving it from text, and Section~\ref{sec:analysis} tests this attribution directly.
Overall, \method's strong results on AppWorld highlight its capability in orchestrating long-horizon application control.

\textbf{Capability on Multi-Hop Retrieval and Reasoning.}\quad
We evaluate \method on BrowseComp-Plus, FanOutQA, and FRAMES to assess its \emph{multi-hop retrieval and reasoning}, where an answer depends on composing evidence gathered across many calls.
As shown in Table~\ref{tab:main}, \method improves over both baselines on all three benchmarks for every backbone, and it recovers the collapsed PTC accuracy of Claude-Opus-5 on BrowseComp-Plus to a level above Direct Tool Calling.
This recovery reflects cross-block dependency linking, which marks equivalent results and lets the agent stop re-issuing queries whose content it already holds.
Overall, \method's strong results on these benchmarks highlight its capability in composing multi-hop evidence under programmatic tool calling.

\textbf{Capability on Stateful Workflow Automation.}\quad
We evaluate \method on Agent-Diff and AutomationBench to assess its \emph{stateful workflow automation}, where a task completes only when a chain of dependent write operations all take effect.
As shown in Table~\ref{tab:main}, \method improves over both baselines on both benchmarks for every backbone, and it turns the case where PTC underperforms even Direct Tool Calling on DeepSeek-V4-Pro back into a gain.
Here progress assessment separates a genuine state change from a re-executed no-op, so the agent advances the workflow instead of restating a completed step.
Overall, \method's strong results on Agent-Diff and AutomationBench highlight its capability in stateful workflow automation.

Across the panel, \method improves over both Direct Tool Calling and PTC on every benchmark, by $7.4$ points over PTC and $6.9$ points over Direct Tool Calling on average, with consistent gains on four heterogeneous backbones.
